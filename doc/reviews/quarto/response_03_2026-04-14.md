## Major Comments

### Comment 1: Conceptual framing drifts; throughput can't be both predictor and outcome

**Classification**: VALID --- ACTION TAKEN

**Response**: The Conceptualization section has been rewritten (index.qmd §3.1) to frame "administrative throughput" as a working label for a set of administratively recorded indicators, not a claim about a latent trait. Recovery Performance and Recovery Timeliness are treated strictly as outcomes; they are no longer described as partly constituting throughput. The SEM is now explicitly introduced as an exploratory decomposition, not a latent-variable validation.

The reviewer's claim that Figure 5.1 "depicts something closer to a one-factor governmental capacity model" was investigated and is incorrect: the figure and surrounding text both describe the two-factor specification with Administrative Resources and Administrative Burden Capacity as distinct latent throughput dimensions predicting Recovery Performance and Recovery Timeliness as distinct outcome latent constructs. No figure change was needed.

### Comment 2: Reframe contribution around instability

**Classification**: VALID --- ACTION TAKEN

**Response**: This is now the organizing revision principle.

- **Title**: Changed to *"Measurement Sensitivity in Administrative-Throughput Analysis of CDBG-DR Disaster Recovery: Evidence from a Cross-Sectional SEM and Complementary Survival Model"*. Leads with sensitivity rather than "Evidence from Cross-Sectional SEM".
- **Abstract** (144 words, under PAR 150-word limit): Opens with "Capacity-outcome associations in CDBG-DR disaster recovery administration are highly sensitive to how capacity is operationalized..." and lists the full β ladder (0.266 → 0.105 → 0.132 n.s. → -0.244 → -0.443 → -0.600) before any interpretive framing.
- **Evidence for Practice**: Lead bullet reframed to "Administrative-capacity metrics in CDBG-DR should not be used as benchmarks without sensitivity analysis." Over-strong practice recommendations (surge-capacity teams, intergovernmental coordination) have been dropped per R2 M7.
- **Discussion**: Opening section renamed "Measurement Sensitivity as the Central Finding" and rewritten to lead with the β ladder, QCEW proxy instability, and the financial-ratio bridge sign reversal.
- **Conclusion**: Rewritten to state "The central empirical finding is not a stable capacity-timeliness coefficient but the instability of that coefficient under principled perturbations of measurement and design."

### Comment 3: Operationalization of workload indicators inconsistent

**Classification**: VALID --- BUG FIX

**Response**: Verified by inspection of the estimation code (`src/capacity_sem/models/kaifa_recovered_analysis.py:244`). The main-text equations (formerly `Num_Program / total_employments`) did not match the code, which computes `Num_Program / (avg_employment × E_TOTPOP + ε)` under the default `workload_mode="employment_x_population"`.

- **Fix (index.qmd §3.1)**: Main-text equations now explicitly write out the two-factor denominator with the epsilon offset, and add one sentence of rationale: "The population-interaction denominator rescales the per-capita employment rate back to jurisdiction-level staffing equivalents, so that workload-per-staff values are comparable across jurisdictions of widely different sizes."
- **Appendix A.3 data dictionary**: Updated to clarify that `avg_employment` is a rate per thousand population, that the denominator is `(avg_employment × E_TOTPOP + ε)`, and that the rescaling translates per-capita employment back to jurisdiction-level staffing equivalents.
- **Appendix C.1 sensitivity table**: The "Employment-only denominator" row is now unambiguously labeled as a *sensitivity test alternative*, not the primary.

### Comment 4: QCEW proxy problem may invalidate main SEM result

**Classification**: VALID --- RE-ANALYSIS EXECUTED

**Response**: R2 deferred the nonzero-QCEW subsample sensitivity; R3 re-raised it. It has now been run.

- **Result**: On the 573-row reference SEM sample restricted to the 111 jurisdictions with `avg_employment > 0` (30 state + 81 local), the two-factor SEM converges (CFI = 0.918, RMSEA = 0.079) with β(BurdenCapacity → Timeliness) = 0.132, p = 0.16 (non-significant).
- **Interpretation**: The primary β = 0.266 attenuates by roughly half and loses statistical significance when the proxy denominator has genuine variation. This is consistent with the reviewer's interpretation that the full-sample result is driven by the 80.6% zero-QCEW majority, for which the burden indicators reduce to an epsilon-offset raw program/disaster count rather than a staffing-scaled ratio.
- **Manuscript updates**: New row in @tbl-robustness-summary (main text §5.4) and @tbl-sensitivity (Appendix C.1); new interpretive paragraph in both locations; explicit foregrounding in the Discussion opening (§6.1).

### Comment 5: Not convinced SEM is the right primary tool

**Classification**: VALID --- ACTION TAKEN (reframed rather than demoted)

**Response**: The SEM has not been demoted but its framing has been substantially softened throughout:

- Conceptualization (§3.1) now states explicitly that the SEM is an "exploratory decomposition of administratively recorded indicators, not the validation of a coherent latent trait."
- Measurement-parameters paragraph (§5.2) acknowledges that boundary estimates (loadings fixed at 1.000, zero residual variances) are "consistent with the exploratory rather than confirmatory reading."
- Model-comparison paragraph (§5.1) adds "the AIC/BIC preference for the one-factor model indicates that the two-factor choice is theoretically motivated rather than statistically compelled" and retains the two-factor as "primary" but "not as a validated latent structure."
- Discussion (§6.1) frames the whole SEM output as associational patterns rather than tests of an underlying construct.

Formal demotion (simpler observed-variable analyses as primary) would have required rebuilding all result tables and is flagged as a possible direction for future work rather than executed in this revision.

### Comment 6: SEM↔survival comparison needs bridge analyses

**Classification**: VALID --- ONE BRIDGE EXECUTED, ONE DEFERRED

**Response**: A cross-sectional SEM with financial-ratio capacity indicators has been added as a bridge analysis that holds framework, unit of analysis, outcome, and covariates constant while varying only the capacity operationalization.

- **Result**: β(FinancialCapacity → Timeliness) = -0.600, p = 0.10 (marginal, N = 573, CFI = 0.926, RMSEA = 0.073). Sign-reversal relative to the staffing-based primary specification.
- **Interpretation**: The sign of the capacity-timeliness association is not a property of the SEM framework; it is a property of how capacity is operationalized. Adding this bridge converts the cross-framework comparison from "suggestive" to partially diagnostic.
- **Manuscript updates**: New row in @tbl-robustness-summary and @tbl-sensitivity; new interpretive paragraph in Discussion §6.1; Limitations §8 explicitly flags that the reverse bridge (Cox model using staffing/workload indicators) would further sharpen the comparison and is recommended as future work.

### Comment 7: Sample construction, censoring, and maturity need sharper treatment

**Classification**: PARTIAL --- ACTION TAKEN; fixed-horizon outcomes deferred

**Response**:

- **Data section**: Already documents the 95% threshold, complete-case exclusion, and maturity composition (R1 gain preserved).
- **Limitations**: Explicitly restated that "the SEM sample is constructed through complete-case deletion on [duration] indicators, biasing the sample toward more-mature and more-complete portfolios" and that "a fixed-horizon outcome design---for example, expenditure-completion share at quarter 8, 12, or 16 since first obligation---would yield cohort-comparable outcome measures that do not require observing completion and would substantially reduce maturity confounding." Explicitly flagged as future work.
- Rebuilding the panel around fixed-horizon outcomes is a significant redesign; deferred rather than executed.

### Comment 8: Internal inconsistencies in data-construction narrative (geography matching)

**Classification**: VALID --- BUG FIX

**Response**: The Methods section described "city-to-county lookup tables and manual review of unresolved cases" for the 573-jurisdiction SEM sample, while Appendix A.6 reported 100% match rates with no inference or manual disambiguation needed. Verification of the linking code (`src/stages/s01_link.py`) and the upstream Kaifa bundle confirmed that the broader raw-preprocessing workflow does use lookup tables and manual review, but the 573 profiles that survive complete-case exclusion to enter the SEM all resolve cleanly to Census GEOIDs.

- **Methods rewrite**: Now distinguishes the two stages explicitly: "In the broader upstream workflow used to assemble administrative-jurisdiction profiles, ambiguous labels were resolved through city-to-county lookup tables and manual review..." and "For the 573 administering-jurisdiction profiles that entered the final complete-case SEM sample, the retained labels were those that resolved cleanly..." and "The 100% match rate in Appendix A.6 refers to the surviving 573 profiles, not to the raw QPR universe."
- The two accounts are now reconcilable.
