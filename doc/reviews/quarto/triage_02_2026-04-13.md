### Major 2: Conceptualization of "throughput" not coherent

**Classification**: PARTIALLY ADDRESSED

R1 added "structured data reduction" language and softened "latent construct" to "summary factor." But the Conceptualization section (lines 120-138) still reads as a theoretical framework: "throughput is conceptualized as the institutional ability of public agencies to administer CDBG-DR funds." The reviewer's concern is that throughput sometimes means upstream capacity, sometimes resources + workload, sometimes is jointly reflected by resources AND performance outcomes — blurring predictor and outcome. The reviewer suggests either reframing the SEM as "exploratory index construction" or simplifying to observed composites.

**Action needed (prose)**: Revise the Conceptualization section to frame the SEM explicitly as exploratory decomposition of administratively-recorded indicators, not as validation of a coherent theoretical construct. Clarify that "throughput" is a working label for the set of indicators, not a claim about an underlying generative mechanism.

### Major 5: Remove weak indicator from primary model

**Classification**: VALID — ACTION NEEDED (requires re-analysis)

R1 strengthened the justification for retaining the weak expended/disbursed indicator and noted in B.4 that "Recovery Performance would function equally well as a three-indicator factor." R2 disagrees with the "transparency" rationale: "The main model in the paper should be the most defensible one, not the least edited one." **Action needed (re-analysis)**: Re-estimate the primary SEM with a three-indicator Recovery Performance factor. If results are indeed identical (as the sensitivity test indicates), swap the primary and sensitivity specifications.

### M2: Rename "Administrative Burden Capacity"

**Classification**: VALID — ACTION NEEDED (prose)

Important point: in public administration, "administrative burden" refers to learning, compliance, and psychological costs imposed on citizens (Herd & Moynihan 2019). The manuscript's concept is closer to "workload manageability" or "administrative slack." Renaming would avoid terminological confusion with a well-established literature.

**Note**: This is a global rename that touches many files and all appendix tables. The SEM output labels would also need updating unless the rename is cosmetic (keeping internal variable names but changing manuscript-facing labels).
