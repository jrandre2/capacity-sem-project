## Major Comments

### Comment 1: SEM too fragile to serve as primary inferential engine

**Classification**: PARTIAL — soften without restructuring

R5 offers two options: (a) make observed-variable/fixed-horizon the primary, (b) strengthen the SEM justification. The revision chooses a middle path: the SEM is retained as the primary associational summary while the Appendix C.7 self-undermining language is softened and cluster-appropriate inference is foregrounded in the Robustness section.

- **Appendix C.7 language softened**: the statement that the observed-variable regression is "a more defensible primary analytic stance" has been rewritten. The new framing is: "The observed-variable regression and the SEM are complementary readings of the same associational structure... Both read the same underlying signal; neither is a definitive primary specification on its own." This honors R5's concern that the appendix should not actively contradict the main-paper framing.
- **State-clustered bootstrap already in main text**: @tbl-robustness-summary (§5.4) has the state-clustered bootstrap row since R4. The Robustness narrative was rewritten so the bootstrap CI crossing zero is the *first* pattern discussed, not a later footnote.
- **Acknowledged tradeoff**: the Methods and Discussion sections explicitly frame the SEM as exploratory decomposition rather than latent-variable validation.

### Comment 2: Timeliness outcome built on selected sample

**Classification**: VALID — ADDRESSED (fixed-horizon elevated)

The fixed-horizon outcomes analysis (Appendix C.8) addresses this directly by computing cohort-invariant outcomes (expenditure share at q = 8, 12, 16) that do not require observing completion. The new design matrix (§4.3, @tbl-design-matrix) makes the selection rule explicit across all designs. The Limitations section (§8, item 6) already flags fixed-horizon at N = 573 as future work. Re-centering the entire paper around fixed-horizon would require rebuilding the QPR panel for all 573 SEM grantees (currently only 45 overlap with QPR data); this is a design change scoped for a future cycle.

### Comment 3: Survival analysis internally inconsistent — **CRITICAL BUG FIXED**

**Classification**: VALID — BUG FIXED

R5 is correct that the previous Appendix C.5 threshold-sensitivity Cox table was inconsistent with the main-text Cox. On investigation:

- The previous appendix table used `Completion_Pct >= threshold` as the event indicator. But `Completion_Pct` in `panel_features_std.parquet` is **not a percentage** — it is a raw accumulation value with mean 726,247 and maximum 112 million. Thresholding raw values at 0.20 or 0.95 is meaningless.
- The HR $\approx$ 0.30 with $p < 0.001$ I had reported was an artifact of the broken event coding and did not represent a meaningful threshold sensitivity.

**Action taken**: the threshold-sensitivity Cox has been rebuilt with the correct event coding. For each threshold $t$, the event is redefined as the first reporting quarter in which the jurisdiction's cumulative expended-to-obligated ratio (computed from `qpr_quarterly.parquet` `QPR Fund Expended Clean $` / `QPR Fund Obligated Clean $`) reaches $t$. Covariates are measured at baseline ($q = 3$) rather than end-of-observation maxima, to avoid outcome leakage. Pairs that had already crossed the threshold by baseline are excluded.

The corrected results show **positive** hazard ratios on baseline disbursement across thresholds (HR = 1.5–2.9, $p < 0.001$ across most thresholds), not negative. Jurisdictions with higher baseline disbursement ratios complete faster. This is a different substantive message than the main-text time-varying Cox (HR $\approx$ 1.0, null). The reconciliation: the two specifications ask different questions:

- **Main-text time-varying Cox**: "Does a change in the lagged disbursement ratio affect next-quarter hazard?" Answer: no, HR $\approx$ 1.
- **Appendix single-spell Cox**: "Does the level of the disbursement ratio at baseline predict eventual time to completion?" Answer: yes, positively.

Both answers are valid. The time-varying null is about dynamics (marginal changes quarter-to-quarter); the single-spell positive is about levels (early differences between jurisdictions). A new reconciliation subsection in Appendix C.5 explains this explicitly.

**Event-count reconciliation (70 vs 105)**: The main-text Cox draws events from `panel_time_varying.parquet`'s `E` column (70 events at 95% threshold). The buggy appendix table reported 105 events from the broken `Completion_Pct` threshold; the corrected appendix table reports 20 events at the 95% threshold from N = 109 pairs after excluding those that had already crossed the threshold by baseline. Both event counts are correct under their respective definitions.

### Comment 4: Construct contamination in bridge analyses

**Classification**: VALID — ADDRESSED

R5 is right to flag that the disbursed-to-obligated and expended-to-disbursed ratios serve as Recovery Performance indicators in the primary SEM measurement model but are re-cast as capacity indicators in the within-SEM financial-ratio bridge. The new paragraph in Appendix C.1 acknowledges this:

> A conceptual caveat applies to the financial-ratio bridge specifically: the same disbursed-to-obligated and expended-to-disbursed ratios serve as Recovery Performance indicators in the primary SEM measurement model, but are re-cast as capacity indicators in this bridge. Two interpretations are possible. Under a strict construct-separation view, these ratios are outcomes only and their use as predictors in the bridge represents construct contamination rather than an alternative capacity operationalization. Under an operationalization-alternative view, financial-flow ratios can legitimately proxy administrative capacity (cf. the broader CDBG-DR literature that treats spending rates as throughput measures). The bridge result reported here should be interpreted under the second view while acknowledging that a portion of the sign reversal likely reflects the conceptual shift from outcome to predictor rather than a pure measurement difference. The raw-portfolio-counts bridge (row 14) is not subject to this contamination concern, because raw counts never appear as outcome indicators. The sign reversal on row 14 is therefore evidence that operationalization alone can flip the substantive sign.
