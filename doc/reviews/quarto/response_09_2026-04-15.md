We thank the reviewer for the ninth and sharpest critique in this sequence. R9 correctly identifies that the normative superstructure added in response to R7 (decision rule, Class taxonomy, benchmark-ready verdict) was not adequately grounded: its 50% magnitude cutoff is pegged to a reference coefficient the paper itself argues is unstable, and the classification treats heterogeneous perturbations as comparable "tests." ## Major Comments

### Comment 1: Decision rule too ad hoc; baseline-contamination

**Status**: VALID — RESOLVED via dashboard reframe

The pass/fail "benchmark-ready" verdict introduced in response to R7 has been demoted. §4.1 is rewritten as a specification-curve dashboard with 🟢/🟡/🔴 stability flags annotating each audit row (🟢 = sign retained + magnitude ≥ 50% of reference; 🟡 = attenuated; 🔴 = sign reversed). The 5/6 and 50% conventions are retained as annotation thresholds, not as quasi-objective verdict boundaries. Readers apply their own fitness-for-use criterion over the flag distribution. @tbl-robustness-summary and @tbl-sensitivity (Appendix C.1) both have the new Flag column. Abstract, EfP, §5.4, §6.1, and Conclusion no longer contain a "benchmark-ready" verdict in the R7/R8 form.

### Comment 2: QCEW suppression is first-order; demote pooled ε-coded SEM

**Status**: VALID — RESOLVED

§6.1 is rewritten to lead with the substantive reading that the capacity-timeliness association is near zero once identifiable measurement problems are addressed in any single analytical design: the non-suppressed-QCEW SEM (N=111) yields β = 0.132 (n.s.); the time-varying Cox (N=151) yields HR ≈ 1.0; the fixed-horizon regression attenuates beyond the early-horizon window. The pooled ε-coded SEM's β = 0.266 is explicitly reframed as "less independent evidence than illustration of what happens when BLS-suppressed QCEW zeros are handled by ε-offset." The abstract now leads substantive claims with the N=111 result, not the pooled 0.266. Appendix A.3 adds an explicit terminology block distinguishing literal-zero, BLS-suppressed, and non-suppressed-nonzero observations. Tobit-style censored-data SEM (R9's strong form) is not supported by semopy and is documented as future work.

### Comment 3: Reverse analytic hierarchy; SEM secondary/illustrative

**Status**: PARTIAL — RESOLVED via prose demotion (strong form deferred)

§5 Results opening is rewritten to explicitly position the cross-sectional SEM as "the most specification-sensitive design" and "illustrative entry point into the dashboard," not the preferred inferential engine. The less-compromised designs (nonzero-QCEW SEM, time-varying Cox, fixed-horizon regression) are foregrounded in §6.1 and Conclusion. Full-sample fixed-horizon rebuild is not feasible in this revision because the upstream ARO→jurisdiction linking for the full 573 is not independently re-derivable from public data alone; that limitation is documented in Appendix A.5 Reproducibility Boundary.

### Comment 4: SEM not persuasive enough; simplify to exploratory

**Status**: PARTIAL — EXISTING FRAMING REINFORCED

The §5.4 observed-composite subsection already documents that the observed-variable coefficient (b = +0.276) is nearly identical to the latent SEM coefficient (β = +0.266), so the SEM functions primarily as dimension reduction. §4.3 already frames the SEM as "exploratory decomposition, not validation of a coherent latent trait." Full replacement of the SEM by the observed-composite as the primary specification is documented as future work.

### Comment 5: Pooled SEM ≠ valid shared construct

**Status**: VALID — RESOLVED via §5.3 prose qualification

§5.3 now contains an explicit paragraph distinguishing "robustness to population composition" (what the pooled/local-only similarity demonstrates) from "invariance of the latent construct" (not testable at N = 30 state agencies). The pooled model is now described as "a cross-sectional summary pattern that reproduces on the local subsample, not as evidence of a shared capacity construct across government types."

### Comment 6: Cross-framework overinterpreted

**Status**: ADDRESSED in R8

§6.3 tone was tightened in the R8 cycle to describe cross-framework triangulation as "illustrative" rather than adjudicative; §5.5 surfaces the within-Cox divergence explicitly. No further changes in this cycle.

### Comment 7: Deposit crosswalk; sharpen reproducibility limits

**Status**: VALID — RESOLVED

A new script (`scripts/export_jurisdiction_crosswalk.py`) produces `data_work/replication/jurisdiction_crosswalk.csv` (573 rows: Grantee label, state_level, state abbreviation, inferred geography type, county name where applicable, linker ARO-count metadata). Appendix A.5 gains a new "Reproducibility Boundary" subsection explicitly distinguishing fully-reproducible workflow components (linker reconstruction, bootstrap, NDR/MIT exclusion, local-only SEM, fixed-horizon, figure generation) from the partly-reproducible upstream manual adjudication (1,708 unresolved labels that cannot be re-derived from public data alone; the final 573-case crosswalk is deposited so downstream analyses are reproducible even though the upstream ARO-to-jurisdiction assignment is not). The Data Availability Statement explicitly cross-references the crosswalk.

### Comment 8: Overlong; abstract duplication; notation

**Status**: PARTIAL

Abstract compressed to 146 words (within 150-word PAR limit). Notation complaints in §6.2 and Appendix C.7 — verified across R3–R9 as Quarto→DOCX rendering artifact affecting inline math delimiters; the LaTeX source is correct and the HTML/PDF builds render cleanly.
