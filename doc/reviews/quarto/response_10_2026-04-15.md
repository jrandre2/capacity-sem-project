## Major Comments

### Comment 1: "Near zero" central claim is itself overreach

**Status**: VALID — RECAST

R10 is correct: the R9 revision elevated the measurement-appropriate specifications' near-zero reading to a substantive verdict, but the dashboard contains positive (0.266, 0.297, 0.257), near-zero (0.132 n.s., $\approx 0$ at fixed horizons, HR ≈ 1.0), and negative (−0.244, −0.443, −0.600) estimates. Privileging the near-zero slice was selective. The Abstract, §6.1, and Conclusion are rewritten to claim **"the capacity-timeliness coefficient is not stably identified under these measurement choices"** and **"current capacity findings in CDBG-DR are not robust enough for benchmarking or policy anchoring."** The full dashboard spread is reported in §5.4 with no privileged interpretation; the measurement-appropriate near-zero reading appears as a sub-finding in §6.1, not as the headline.

### Comment 2: Class Ia mis-classification of fixed-horizon; split dashboard

**Status**: VALID — RECLASSIFIED

Fixed-horizon rows are now Class **II-O** (outcome change), not Ia. The class taxonomy in §4.1 introduces Class II-C (capacity-operationalization change) vs. II-O (outcome change). @tbl-robustness-summary, @tbl-sensitivity (Appendix C.1), and @fig-spec-curve all relabel accordingly: raw-portfolio-counts → II-C; financial-ratio bridge → II-C* (construct reassignment); reconstructed-panel fixed-horizon q=8/12/16 → II-O. The figure has been regenerated with II-O (orange) and II-C (red) as distinct colors. §5.4 prose explicitly notes that II-O coefficients estimate a different estimand than the SEM Class Ia/Ib rows and do not directly adjudicate the SEM capacity-timeliness estimate.

### Comment 3: QCEW strengthening — four asks

**Status**: VALID — RESOLVED

(a) **Exact ε in main text:** §4.3.2 now states "$\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$" in the body prose and explains that the ε *value* is analytically inconsequential within the ε-offset family because z-standardization removes the ε scaling.

(b) **ε-sensitivity scan:** New script (`scripts/run_epsilon_sensitivity.py`) refits the SEM at $\varepsilon \in \{10^{-3}, 10^{-6}, 10^{-9}, 10^{-12}\}$. Result: standardized coefficients are invariant to within numerical precision (Burden → Timeliness = +0.267 across all ε values). This is reported in Appendix C.10 alongside the bounds analysis. The substantive conclusion: ε *value* doesn't matter; the *treatment* of suppressed zeros as if they were literal zeros does.

(c) **Imputation-range units justification:** Appendix C.10 now translates the BLS suppression-floor convention (cells suppressed at <3 establishments or ≤10 employees) into the units of the derived `avg_employment` variable (employees per thousand population). For the median zero-QCEW jurisdiction (population = 65K), 3 employees corresponds to ~0.046 emp./K pop, 10 to ~0.15 emp./K pop. The 0.25–10 imputation range therefore brackets the plausible suppression-cell magnitudes from a few employees per small county to dozens per medium county to hundreds per large county.

(d) **Zero-QCEW vs. nonzero-QCEW transportability comparison:** New script (`scripts/build_zero_nonzero_comparison.py`) and new table @tbl-zero-nonzero-comparison in Appendix A.3. The non-suppressed subsample is *not* transportable to the suppressed majority — it has 40× larger populations, 27% state agencies (vs. 0%), 5× more programs, 12 months shorter program completion, and *lower* fully-expended/completion ratios. The R9 normative claim that "substantive claims should rest on the non-suppressed subsample" is appropriately walked back: the non-suppressed subsample describes a specific population segment, not the broader CDBG-DR-administering population. This walkback is reflected in §6.1 and the new EfP language about specification-curve reading.

### Comment 4: SEM repositioning + bootstrap CIs in main table

**Status**: VALID — TABLE EXPANDED

@tbl-structural now includes a **cluster-bootstrap 95% CI column** (1,000 iterations, state-level clustering, pooled frame) for the four capacity-related paths. The CI for the headline AdminBurdenCapacity → RecoveryTimeliness path is [−0.129, 0.531] — crosses zero, surfacing the inference-cluster sensitivity directly in the main text rather than requiring a reader to consult Appendix C.6. The SEM continues to be framed as an "exploratory decomposition, not a validated latent structure" per the existing prose; we did not move the observed-variable model to the main specification (R10's stronger ask) because the existing §5.4 observed-composite subsection already shows the latent SEM is functioning primarily as dimension reduction.

### Comment 5: Main-text included-vs-excluded sample table

**Status**: VALID — NEW TABLE

New script (`scripts/build_sample_selection_comparison.py`) and new table @tbl-incl-excl in Appendix A.5 distinguish included (county/city-resolvable, N=995 AROs at the F31 level), excluded-institutional (N=444), and excluded-unresolved (N=1,708) categories with median F31-row counts, dollar-volume aggregates, and disaster-context counts. §3.2 Data Cleaning has a new sentence cross-referencing the table and explicitly noting that the SEM sample includes local jurisdictions whose F31 labels resolve cleanly to a county or city plus all state agencies, and does not include CDBG-DR administration that flows through non-geographic institutional entities. Appendix A.5 prose qualifies the table with two caveats: F31 dollar volumes in excluded categories are deceptively large because many institutional flows are aggregated upstream into state-agency profiles, and the unresolved category is heterogeneous.

### Comment 6: Temporal meaning of "capacity"

**Status**: VALID — ELEVATED TO MAIN TEXT

§4.3.2 (Conceptualization of Administrative Capacity) now contains an explicit paragraph noting that QCEW indicators are computed across the years each jurisdiction had active CDBG-DR grants — *contemporaneous*, not pre-existing or baseline capacity — and that the proxy may co-evolve with the workload and outcome measures. Audit results in §5.4 should not be read as estimating an effect of pre-existing capacity. Baseline/lagged QCEW sensitivity is flagged as future work, with a note that baseline-year values inherit the same suppression structure and would not resolve the suppression problem on their own.

### Comment 7: Cox evidence asymmetry in abstract

**Status**: VALID — REWORDED

Abstract now reads: **"The companion Cox is itself measurement-sensitive: null under time-varying specification, positive and significant under baseline specification."** Conclusion has parallel language. The asymmetry between time-varying and single-spell baseline Cox is no longer obscured.
