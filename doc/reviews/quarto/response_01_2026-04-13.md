## Major Comments

### Comment 1: Reframe around measurement sensitivity

**Classification**: VALID --- ACTION TAKEN

**Response**: We agree that the paper's most durable contribution is the demonstration that capacity-outcome associations are highly sensitive to operationalization, maturity, and analytical framework. The revision restructures the manuscript accordingly:

- **Abstract** (index.qmd, line 28): Now opens with "Capacity-outcome associations in disaster recovery administration are highly sensitive to..." and lists the specific attenuated and reversed betas (0.105, -0.244, -0.443) before reporting the primary estimate.
- **Contributions** (index.qmd, Introduction): Measurement sensitivity is now contribution #1: "it demonstrates that capacity-outcome associations in CDBG-DR are highly sensitive to how capacity is operationalized..."
- **Discussion** (index.qmd, line 270): Section renamed "Measurement Sensitivity as the Central Finding" and rewritten to lead with "The most important finding from this analysis is not a single stable coefficient but rather the degree to which capacity-outcome associations depend on measurement and modeling choices."
- **Conclusion** (index.qmd): Rewritten to center the sensitivity finding rather than the positive beta.
- **Evidence for Practice** (index.qmd, lines 49--52): Lead bullet now foregrounds measurement caution: "Capacity-outcome associations in CDBG-DR are highly sensitive to measurement choices: the same data can show positive, null, or negative associations..."

### Comment 2: QCEW proxy problem is central

**Classification**: VALID --- ACTION TAKEN

**Response**: The reviewer specifically asked how programs_per_staff and disasters_per_staff are computed when QCEW employment is zero. We traced the computation to the estimation code and found that an epsilon offset (10^-6) is added to the denominator. This means that for the 85.1% of local jurisdictions with zero QCEW employment, the burden indicators are effectively proportional to raw program counts rather than staffing-scaled ratios. This is now explicitly documented:

- **Appendix A.4** (appendix-a-data.qmd, line 67): New "Zero-employment handling in burden indicators" section explains the epsilon offset, its distributional consequences (the zero-employment majority dominates the distribution), and its connection to the sign reversal in Appendix C.6.
- **Limitations** (index.qmd): New sentence in the third limitation paragraph: "For jurisdictions with zero QCEW employment, the burden indicators...are computed with an epsilon offset in the denominator and are effectively proportional to raw program counts rather than staffing-scaled ratios."

### Comment 4: Recovery Timeliness is maturity-dependent

**Classification**: VALID --- ACTION TAKEN

**Response**: The reviewer asked how duration was defined for incomplete portfolios. The revision now explicitly states:

- **Outcome section** (index.qmd, line 150): "Duration of completion is defined as the number of months from first obligation to the quarter in which a jurisdiction reaches 95% of obligated funds expended. Jurisdictions whose portfolios have not reached 95% expenditure are treated as incomplete; for the cross-sectional SEM, these cases are excluded through complete-case deletion on the timeliness indicators, biasing the SEM sample toward more-mature and more-complete portfolios."
- The maturity composition is quantified (N = 110 young, N = 206 mature) with a cross-reference to Appendix C.3.

### Comment 5: Survival analysis is not a true robustness test

**Classification**: VALID --- ACTION TAKEN

**Response**: We agree that because four design elements change simultaneously (unit of analysis, outcome, capacity measure, time handling), the survival analysis cannot directly test whether the SEM relationship survives a better temporal specification. Changes made:

- **Main text** (index.qmd, line 170): "robustness check" replaced with "complementary analytical framework" / "companion framework."
- **Appendix B.6** (appendix-b-methods.qmd, line 156): New "Proportional hazards diagnostics" paragraph documenting Schoenfeld, martingale, and Cox-Snell residual tests.
- **Appendix B.6** (appendix-b-methods.qmd, line 158): New "Power considerations" paragraph: "With only 40 completion events among 152 grantee-disaster observations and multiple covariates, the survival analysis has limited statistical power to detect moderate effects."
- **Appendix B.6** (appendix-b-methods.qmd, line 160): Explicitly states: "The survival analysis is therefore best understood as a complementary companion framework rather than as a robustness check of the SEM."
- **Results** (index.qmd): Power caveat added to the survival results paragraph.

### Comment 6: Pooled state/local SEM not convincing as national model

**Classification**: VALID --- ACTION TAKEN

**Response**: The revision scales back pooled-sample language and flags the SE limitation:

- **Methods** (index.qmd, line 118): "national SEM" replaced with "broad SEM." Added: "the structural findings therefore primarily reflect local-jurisdiction variation. Standard errors are conventional ML and do not account for within-state clustering, likely understating uncertainty."
- This complements the existing Appendix C.5 stratified results (state-only degenerate, local-only reproduces pooled beta).

We did not implement cluster-robust SEs because the semopy estimation library does not currently support them. The limitation is now disclosed in both the methods section and the Limitations.

### Comment 8: Recovery Performance should be re-specified

**Classification**: VALID --- ACTION TAKEN (justification strengthened; re-estimation deferred)

**Response**: The reviewer suggested dropping the weak expended/disbursed indicator or decomposing outcomes. Because the sensitivity test already shows that dropping the indicator leaves all structural paths unchanged (Appendix C.1), and because retaining it shows the full measurement structure honestly, we retain it but substantially strengthen the justification:

- **Appendix B.4** (appendix-b-methods.qmd): Expanded discussion explicitly notes that Recovery Performance "would function equally well (and more coherently) as a three-indicator factor" and recommends that "future work treat flow-through, completion, and utilization as distinct observed outcomes rather than forcing them into a single summary factor."

Re-estimation with a three-indicator primary specification is deferred to the next revision cycle, as it requires re-running the SEM pipeline and updating all tables. The current sensitivity check confirms that results would be identical.
