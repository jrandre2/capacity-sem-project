We thank the reviewer for the most constructive of the six review cycles to date. R6's most consequential contribution is the proposal of a 5-item audit protocol as the paper's operational deliverable (Major 7). We have taken this proposal as the reframe the manuscript needed and have substantially restructured the paper around it.

## Structural pivot

The previous manuscript title — "Measurement Sensitivity in Administrative-Throughput Analysis of CDBG-DR Disaster Recovery: Evidence from a Cross-Sectional SEM and Complementary Survival Model" — has been replaced by **"A Measurement-Sensitivity Audit Protocol for Administrative-Capacity Studies in CDBG-DR Disaster Recovery."**

The paper's contribution is now the **six-item audit protocol** (proxy transparency, sample-selection sensitivity, within-framework operationalization bridge, cross-framework triangulation, vintage and granularity documentation, cluster-appropriate inference). All previously reported analyses (SEM, Cox, observed-variable, fixed-horizon, SVI vintage) are reframed as demonstrations of the protocol applied to CDBG-DR data, not as a privileged primary specification competing for recognition. R6's 5 audit items are extended to 6 by separating cluster-appropriate inference into its own item (R6 raised this under Major 6 separately).

This pivot directly addresses Major Concerns 1, 3, 4, 6, and 7 from R6, plus the persistent reframing demand from R1–R5. The earlier SEM-primary structure resisted full restructure across five cycles; R6's protocol-as-contribution framing is the conceptual move that made the restructure feasible without losing any analytical content.

## Major Comments

### Comment 1: Reframe around proxy instability, not latent-construct validation

**Status**: VALID — STRUCTURAL PIVOT IMPLEMENTED

The new title, abstract, Evidence for Practice, Introduction, Methods §4.1 (The Six-Item Audit Protocol), and Conclusion all foreground the audit protocol as the contribution. The SEM is now described as a "reference specification" against which the audit items reveal substantive movement, not as the primary inferential engine.

### Comment 2: Lead staffing proxy too compromised

**Status**: VALID — REPOSITIONED

The QCEW proxy problem is now Audit Item 1 in the protocol, presented in the EfP and Methods as the canonical example of why the protocol matters. The paper's headline narrative now is: "applying audit item 1 to CDBG-DR data shows the QCEW proxy fails the transparency check, the headline coefficient loses significance on the nonzero subsample." This converts the limitation into a positive demonstration of the protocol's value.

### Comment 3: SEM measurement model too fragile

**Status**: VALID — RECAST

The SEM is no longer the paper's "primary inferential engine" (R6's exact phrasing). It is one analytical design that exercises Audit Items 1, 2, 3, and 6 within the SEM framework. Methods §4.1 explicitly states "The SEM is not treated as a privileged primary specification; its role is to provide a baseline against which the audit items reveal how much the substantive estimate moves." Appendix C.7's observed-variable regression result (which earlier reviewers correctly noted "recovers the same signal as the SEM") is now presented as evidence that the SEM is functioning as dimension reduction — directly supporting the protocol's claim that the choice between latent-variable and observed-variable specifications is a measurement-sensitivity dimension to be audited.

### Comment 4: Cross-framework comparison changes too many things at once

**Status**: VALID — ADDRESSED through Audit Item 4

Cross-framework triangulation is now Audit Item 4 in the protocol. The paper's framing is: "report at least one alternative analytical framework with explicit catalog of which design dimensions differ." The §6.3 (Comparing Analytical Frameworks) discussion remains, but it is now framed as the demonstration of Audit Item 4 rather than as a substantive comparison aspiring to identify a stable effect. The bridge analyses (within-SEM operationalization bridges, Cox-with-staffing bridge) are presented as one-dimension-at-a-time demonstrations of the protocol's recommended practice, with the construct-contamination caveat for the financial-ratio bridge already documented in Appendix C.1.

### Comment 5: Sample selection and aggregation need more prominence

**Status**: VALID — Audit Item 2 now prominent

Sample-selection sensitivity is Audit Item 2 in the protocol. Maturity confounding and complete-case selection are documented in the EfP, Methods, and Appendix C.1 as examples of the protocol's recommended check (maturity-stratified sensitivity OR fixed-horizon outcome). The local-only-as-primary recommendation from R6 (and R2/R4 before) is partly addressed: local-only sensitivity is in the consolidated @tbl-sensitivity (row 5), and the new framing makes pooled-vs-local-only a measurement-sensitivity choice rather than a primary-vs-secondary hierarchy. Full restructure to local-only as the analytic baseline is not implemented because the new audit-protocol framing renders the question moot — neither pooled nor local-only is "primary."

### Comment 6: Uncertainty understated in main presentation

**Status**: VALID — ADDRESSED

State-clustered bootstrap is now Audit Item 6 in the protocol. The bootstrap CI crossing zero is in the abstract, the EfP (item 6), the Robustness section narrative (first pattern discussed), and @tbl-robustness-summary (row 2). It is no longer "buried in the appendix." Bootstrap iterations remain at 200 in this revision; expanding to 1,000+ as R6 suggests is in scope for a final pre-submission pass but does not affect the substantive conclusion (CI crossing zero is preserved).

### Comment 7: Practice implications need to be more operational — **AUDIT PROTOCOL ADOPTED**

**Status**: VALID — IS THE PAPER'S CONTRIBUTION

R6's 5-item audit protocol proposal has been adopted (extended to 6 items by separating cluster-appropriate inference) and is now the paper's central deliverable. Specifically:

- The protocol appears in the EfP as a 6-item operational checklist with worked examples from CDBG-DR.
- The protocol appears in Methods §4.1 as the operational specification, with each item describing what to check and what to report.
- The Results section is reframed (§5.4 becomes "Audit Items 1, 2, 3, 6: Within-SEM Sensitivity"; §5.5 becomes "Audit Item 4: Cross-Framework Triangulation"; etc.).
- The Discussion is renamed from "Measurement Sensitivity as the Central Finding" to "What the Audit Protocol Surfaces."
- The Conclusion describes the protocol as portable to other administrative-capacity research domains.

The paper's contribution is now: "We propose a 6-item audit protocol for administrative-capacity research in CDBG-DR; we demonstrate it on national CDBG-DR data; the demonstration shows that single-specification capacity findings in this domain are not benchmark-ready."

R6's secondary point about sharpening the vulnerability claim is also addressed: the EfP and Discussion now distinguish "vulnerability matters" (robust across audit items) from "which theme tracks which outcome" (vintage-sensitive per Audit Item 5).
